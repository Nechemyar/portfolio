# Reference films: how top product and UI films pace interaction

I measured 8 films on 28-29 Oct 2026. Each was downloaded at 720p with yt-dlp, cut points came from ffmpeg scene detection (`select='gt(scene,0.3)'`, with cuts closer than 0.25 s merged), and I read the motion off contact sheets at 1, 4 and 10 fps. The videos and contact sheets are not committed, because they are third-party footage. They're on Nechemya's laptop in `~/sumi-reel/out/ref-films/`. The source URLs are in `sources.md`.

Timings read from the 10 fps sheets are accurate to about ±0.1 s.

## Takeaways (with numbers)

1. **Cut 0.3–0.4 s after the click, not after the result settles.** In Arc Max the cut comes 0.3–0.4 s after each click and the next shot opens on the result. Nobody holds the screen while a list re-sorts or a panel finishes easing. In our takes the gap between action and reaction should be about 1–3 frames, and the editor can cut out the rest.
2. **Cursor travel is short and fast: 0.1–0.4 s from rest to the target, then a 0.3–0.7 s dwell before the click.** The dwell is where the eye lands, and Arc uses it for a hover state such as a tooltip at 0.4 s. Long glides across the screen don't appear in any of the films.
3. **The cursor is big because the frame is cropped, not because the cursor was scaled.** At a 2–2.5x punch-in, a normal cursor fills 4–8% of the frame width. Our oversized cursor is in the right range, provided we crop onto the area being used.
4. **Fast films have a median shot of 0.5–1.2 s** (Screen Studio 0.53, Humaan 0.69, Arc Max 1.16). Explanatory phone films run 2–14 s. For a hero reel aiming at retention, plan 0.8–1.5 s per beat, and give no beat more than about 3 s.
5. **Phone films show a real finger, not a cursor.** All three phone films use a hand. A tap reacts in 2–3 frames, a swipe takes 0.2–0.6 s, and the UI tracks the finger 1:1. If we draw a pointer on phone takes, a round touch dot reads as a finger better than the arrow, but that's a choice for the edit. The cursor logs support either.
6. **Isolate components to show off a feature.** About 40% of Screen Studio's UI time is real panels pulled off the app, floating on a brand gradient and popping in 0.5 s apart. That suits things like the Choice price total, the Settle gauge or the JSense panel, and removes the noise of the whole page.
7. **Whole-page footage works for website showreels.** Humaan is 70% full pages with no cursor at all, cut to the beat at about 1 s per site. The interaction is the site's own motion, such as scroll reveals and hover animations. Interaction with a cursor belongs to app and product films.
8. **Tilted multi-phone mockups on flat brand colour** take about 10% of the Humaan reel. It's the standard way to say "and it's responsive" in about 2 s without a separate phone film.
9. **Transitions are mostly hard cuts.** The exceptions are one long push-in into the screen (Screen Studio, 2.8 s, used once to go from the world into the UI) and short whip-blurs (Arc Search, about 0.3 s). Nobody uses fancy wipes between UI beats.
10. **No film shows a tap or click that changes nothing.** Every input in the films I sampled produces a visible change within 0.1–0.4 s. That backs the brief's rule: drop any input whose result isn't instantly visible.

## What this means for our takes

- Phone takes: real touch input, a reaction within 1–3 frames, about 0.35–0.5 s before the next input, and no taps on labels.
- Pre-scroll so that every target is already on screen. The longest single beat should be about 2 s, at the slider drag or a hold-to-confirm.
- The editor can crop each beat 1.5–2.5x onto the area in use and cut 0.3–0.4 s after each input.
